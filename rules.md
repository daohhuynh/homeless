# Generator rules

What the generator must do to produce a place rather than an arrangement.

Not design. Design says what the city is for; this says what makes a piece of it
read as real. Rules enter here only by surviving the Gallery: a rule is written
when a batch of variants is rated and the reason a group of them sank can be
named. Every rule is therefore attached to the batch that produced it.

A rule that cannot be stated as something the generator does differently does
not belong here. "Too tidy" is a verdict; "buildings on a block must not share a
height" is a rule.

## Standing rules

Carried in from observation rather than from a rated batch. Marked as such,
and the first batch that touches one either confirms or kills it.

- **A room is organized around its use and its entrance, not around its
  contents.** Source: the oneshot build. Its office had chairs lined against all
  four walls and a desk in the middle, which is what you get from satisfying a
  description rather than modeling a use. A room needs a public side and a staff
  side, something facing the door, somewhere the queue stands, and dead floor
  nobody uses. The failure is not density and cannot be fixed by adding or
  removing furniture. Unrated.

- **Interiors are judged entered from outside.** Whether a room makes sense is
  mostly decided by what is visible in the first second through the door, so a
  variant spawned into is a variant judged wrong. Unrated.

- **Placement by rule reads as filler.** One named location per block on the
  widest lot is a rule, and real cities have no such rule. Anything the eye can
  reverse-engineer into a rule stops reading as a place. Unrated.

## Open axes

Things suspected to matter, not yet varied in a batch.

- Height variation between neighbouring buildings.
- Rotation off the grid.
- Clutter density at ankle height.
- Setback depth and its variation along a street.
- Gaps: empty lots, a building that is only a wall.
- Wear decal density and placement.
- Elevation change across a street.

# Homeless co-op survival game

**Status: Q1, Q2, and Q3 answered.** The win, the target, the deadline, the
clock, and the verb set are settled. Q4 is next: what each route costs, and in
what currency.

Reasoning, findings, rejected ideas, and hypotheses live in RECORD.md. Build
facts live in CODEBASE.md. What makes a generated place read as real lives in
rules.md, which is written by rating variants rather than by argument. None of
them is design.

## Objective

**面白い.** Interesting, not fun, not balanced, not fair. A disaster can be
面白い. A tuned economy usually isn't.

Not valid as objections to anything here: "is this balanced," "is this
optimal," "is this fair." Valid: "this produces one shape of decision," "nobody
would see this happen," "this can't be talked about afterward."

Adjudicated by Dao. Not appealable to a model, including in autonomous
sessions.

## Pillars

- **Straight-faced systems, unserious players.** The game never jokes. Comedy
  comes from coordination failure, never from mocking the condition.
- **Starting conditions are unfair on purpose.** The group is the balancing
  mechanism, not the dealer.

## Premise

Four friends, homeless, unequal starting hands, a deadline.

## Decided

Each carries its reason. Reopen by attacking the reason.

- **Housing is not terminal.** Rent recurs, residential rules bind, housing can
  be lost. Reason: a player with nothing left to lose has no stakes, and
  generosity from a player with no stakes is free, which collapses the choice of
  whether to help into no choice.

- **The target is the count of housed players.** 0/4 to 4/4, visible to
  everyone at all times. Reason: a shared target has to be one number everyone
  can look at, or a player's success is something the others have to be told
  about afterward. Because housing is not terminal, the count can go down, which
  a quota cannot.

- **Housing gates on eligibility, not price.** A lease requires verifiable
  income, not cash. A job requires a fixed address. So the chain is closed to
  all four at minute zero and opens only when someone gets in through a worse
  door. Reason: this is what kills the degenerate line where all four hoard cash
  and sign four leases on the last day. Cash is not sufficient, so someone has
  to go first and pay for everyone else's entry.

- **Housing has a lead time.** Application, checks, references, inspection,
  lease start. Reason: the operative deadline becomes the last safe start, not
  the end of the run, so waiting can make the win unreachable rather than
  merely expensive.

- **Housing supply is finite and contested by NPCs.** Cheap units disappear over
  the run. Reason: adds race pressure against hoarding, and puts the four
  players in competition for the same listings, which produces sabotage nobody
  has to design.

- **Cash is unsafe and it drains.** No bank account without ID and an address,
  so savings are carried. Daily survival costs money. Reason: hoarding becomes a
  visible strategic position the others can see and act on, rather than a safe
  default. There is no AFK, only a lower burn rate.

- **Starts deal two things per player, from separate pools, independently, with
  no point values.** A good roll qualifies for the pool only if other players
  want it from you. A bad roll qualifies only if it forces you to ask someone.
  Reason: one dealt difference each splits the table into players who only have
  offers and players who only have needs. Two gives every player both a seat in
  the negotiation and a reason to enter one from the weak side. Values are
  unnecessary because the qualification test already excludes dead entries, and
  a good roll is worth something to the group whether or not its owner can use
  it.

- **Time is a real diegetic clock, roughly 6am to 10pm.** Actions cost
  game-minutes; travel costs whatever the distance costs. Design numbers are
  written in game-hours, and exactly one constant is deferred: game-minutes per
  real second. Reason: nothing may be derived from measured walking time in an
  unfinished city, but the fix is to defer one conversion constant, not to
  abstract the clock away. Tuning that constant later leaves every design number
  above it intact. Slots were considered and rejected; see RECORD.md.

- **Night is a real state and institutions are shut.** Reason: it makes daytime
  belong to the housed and the eligible, and night belong to the player with
  nothing left to lose. Every route with a downside is a night route, so the
  day/night cycle enforces subtracted verbs for free.

- **Sleep advances the clock, and the players choose when.** Reason: a day spent
  waiting on an application costs almost no real time because nobody has
  anything to do, so a sixteen-day run can fit one sitting. Institutional
  latency is what makes days empty, and empty days are cheap. It also makes
  bedtime a nightly negotiation between players with different deadlines.

- **The clock is shared, and each sleeping player accelerates it.** Nobody skips
  the night and nobody is separated in time; a player who beds down speeds the
  clock for whoever is still awake. Reason: going to bed becomes something you do
  *to* the others while being able to say you were only tired, which is sabotage
  with no verb, no button, and no bad intent. It also grades the pressure, so one
  sleeper and three sleepers are different situations, and it squeezes the last
  unhoused player at exactly the point the run is tensest. Separation in time was
  rejected because a sleeping player would be absent rather than a witness, while
  separation in space preserves witnessing: a player across town is still on
  voice and can still arrive too late. Bedtime remains a nightly refusal with a
  price on both sides, since staying up costs sleep quality, which corrupts
  tomorrow. The rate, and whether it compounds, is not decided; see RECORD.md.

- **Interiors are continuous with the street, and doors are Move.** You walk in.
  The door opens as you pass through it and shuts behind you, there is no
  transition, and the room is real geometry inside the building's own footprint
  rather than a pocket reached by a teleport. Reason: a door on an interact key
  is a sixth verb, and the verb set is a hard ceiling. Consequences: windows
  work, so whether an office is open and whether there is a queue are things you
  learn by looking rather than by entering, which is information the city owes a
  player whose whole day is spent judging which trip is worth the hours. A shut
  door must therefore be genuinely shut, with its hours legible from outside,
  which makes a closed institution a Read. Not every building opens; most are
  somebody else's home or business and are locked, and that is what makes the
  named locations legible.

- **Night routes reuse the daytime city under different rules.** Squatting a
  building you could not rent, sleeping somewhere you will be moved along from,
  taking from a place you would otherwise be a customer of. Reason: not cost.
  A route that recurs and reuses a system produces situations every run, where a
  bespoke set piece produces one.

- **The deadline is an announced institutional event, shared by all four.**
  Working version: the city posts notice that the encampment is cleared on the
  16th. Reason: institutional because a clock cannot be blamed and a city can;
  announced because a deadline you can plan against is pressure while a surprise
  is only a loss; shared because it is the only thing that makes the last twenty
  minutes loud for everyone at once. A per-player debt date is a private pressure
  layered on top, not a substitute.

- **Deadline length is derived from the pipeline, not chosen.** Minimum viable
  chain plus slack for exactly one failure. Sketch: address day 1-2, apply day 3,
  hired day 4, first verifiable income day 8 if pay is weekly, lease application
  day 9, processing three days, approved day 12, move in day 13. One failure
  costs three or four days. Sixteen holds. Perfect play finishes with room; two
  setbacks are desperate. Random within a bounded range is fine as long as the
  floor clears the chain.

- **The verb set is five: Move, Read, Carry/Give, Ask, Sleep.** Reason: a verb
  multiplies against every system, so the set is a hard ceiling and a seventh
  entry is expensive in a way a hundredth building is not. What this excludes is
  deliberate. Eating and using are Carry. Working is Ask at a place, then time
  passing. There is no combat verb, which means confronting a line-cutter is a
  social event with no mechanical resolution available.
  - **Move.** The city is the content.
  - **Read.** A verb rather than passive information, because tiredness corrupts
    it and illiteracy deletes it, so the same sign yields different results per
    player and per night. The only verb another player can perform on your
    behalf, which is what makes illiteracy tradeable instead of dead weight. Its
    case rests on being corruptible and deletable; drop either and it collapses
    into Move.
  - **Carry/Give.** Pick up, hold, hand to a person. The transfer layer.
    Without it the only cooperative verb is talking.
  - **Ask.** Request, apply, submit, be checked. The entire institutional layer
    runs on this one verb, and every failure in the eligibility chain is this
    verb refused.
  - **Sleep.** Commit the night to a place. Sets tomorrow. Accelerates the shared
    clock for everyone still awake.

- **Tiredness has no number, and is legible by three means.** Cause: you know
  you slept in the car, so you know today will be bad, which is what makes the
  choice matter when you make it. Inconsistency: corrupted text re-rolls only
  while out of view, so it is stable under a stare and different when you come
  back, which reads as your failure rather than the build's. Disagreement: the
  other three players are the stable truth, so corruption surfaces as a scene
  rather than a UI element. Reason: the finding requires that a player can tell
  the system is running, which is not the same as quantifying it. This also
  retires the earlier claim that corruption needs a second rendering channel.

- **Dealt conditions are private knowledge with optional disclosure.** The
  player learns their own hand and chooses whether to say. Reason: disclosure is
  a decision, and under-reporting is sabotage that requires no verb, no system,
  and no code beyond the information already existing.

## Consequences already known

Not separate decisions. These fall out of the above and should not be
re-litigated as if they were choices.

- **The sacrifice and the landlord are two separate events.** Counting the chain
  showed the first cannot be the second: if the job chain needs an address and
  housing takes thirteen days, nobody else could finish. So the address unlock
  must be cheap and early, available around day 2: a shelter bed, an SRO,
  general delivery, a squat that can receive post. The martyr is whoever takes
  the bad address early and eats what it costs. The first-housed landlord is a
  later, separate event.
- **The three housed players are the most constrained on the board.** Subtracted
  verbs plus recurring rent means they want to help and are structurally least
  able to. The last unhoused player is the only one who can still act freely.
- **3/4 at the deadline is the tensest part of the run, not a failure state.**
  Three rich, cautious, useless players and one desperate one who can do
  anything.

## Questions, in order

Each is undecidable before the one above it.

1. ~~What is the win, and are outcomes separable?~~ Answered above. Remaining
   piece: whether anything persists between runs, which is the only place a
   reward for win quality could live.
2. ~~What is the pressure, and what makes four players split up?~~ Answered:
   institutions, an announced sweep, a shared clock, hostile night. The party
   splits because the four are on different links of the eligibility chain and
   appointments punch holes in different parts of the day. Whether it splits
   *enough* is the negotiation gate's job, not a decision.
3. ~~What are the verbs?~~ Answered: five.
4. **What does each route cost, and in what currency?** Next. Constraint from
   RECORD.md: costs must be non-substitutable, or every route is one strategy in
   four costumes.
5. **What content exists?** Routes, institutions, starts.
6. **What do the two gates require in order to pass?** Neither has a pass
   condition, so neither can currently fail.
